# NEXAHEDRON — About Page Editorial Review

## Current strengths

The opening is excellent:

> Information already exists.
> Orientation is what is missing.

It names the problem in plain language and creates immediate interest without
requiring prior knowledge. It should remain unchanged.

The page already has the right visual character for Version 1. Large headings,
generous whitespace, restrained color, short passages, and clear horizontal
boundaries make it feel considered rather than promotional. The reading width
is comfortable, the numbered section establishes continuity, and the durable
principles work especially well as a quiet editorial table.

Two sections are already strong:

- “Finding is not the same as understanding” clearly explains why orientation
  matters.
- “Contribution begins with attention” frames participation as careful
  observation rather than software contribution alone.

The page also preserves the most important constitutional claims: Human
judgment remains Human, evidence and uncertainty remain visible, and software
does not borrow authority.

## Visitor questions

The current page answers “Why Orientation?” and “What principles guide this
place?” before it adequately answers “What is this project?”

After the opening paragraph, a first-time visitor can infer that NEXAH helps
people find their bearings and that NEXAHEDRON is a place to experience it.
They still cannot explain:

- what makes NEXAH an ecosystem rather than a single website or product;
- what ORION is or why it exists;
- how ORION differs from NEXAHEDRON;
- what a person actually does in NEXAHEDRON;
- where to continue if they want understanding rather than repository access.

ORION is absent from the page. This leaves the distinction between the
deterministic Core and the Human-facing Workspace invisible. The sentence
“NEXAHEDRON is the first public place to experience that orientation” is also
too compressed: it does not name the Workspace, the bounded session, or the
Human-owned Orientation Record.

The current invitation offers useful actions, but its first action leads to the
implementation repository. For a general About page, the Human experience
should come before source inspection.

## Recommended narrative

### 01 — The missing condition

**Question answered:** Why does this exist?

Keep the current opening headline. Retain one short introductory paragraph that
states the problem and keeps Human judgment visible. Do not introduce ORION or
repository terminology in the opening.

### 02 — What is NEXAH?

**Question answered:** What is the whole project?

Introduce NEXAH as the Orientation Ecosystem. Explain its purpose through
relationships, boundaries, and possible directions. Briefly distinguish it
from AI, search, and knowledge storage without creating a defensive list.

### 03 — What is ORION?

**Question answered:** What does the deterministic Core contribute?

Name ORION plainly as the certified deterministic Core. Explain that it creates
inspectable structural orientation and preserves provenance. End with the
boundary: it does not interpret for the Human or make Human decisions.

### 04 — What is NEXAHEDRON?

**Question answered:** Where does a person encounter the project?

Describe NEXAHEDRON as the Human-facing reference implementation and Workspace.
Make the scale tangible: one person, one question, one bounded Orientation
Session, and one Human-owned Orientation Record.

### 05 — Why Orientation?

**Question answered:** Why are search and summaries insufficient?

Keep the existing “Finding is not the same as understanding” section. It becomes
more effective after the visitor knows what NEXAH, ORION, and NEXAHEDRON are.
Its present paragraph is concise and should remain substantially unchanged.

### 06 — Durable principles

**Question answered:** What constrains the project?

Keep the existing four-row table unchanged. It is the clearest part of the
current page and translates the architecture into Human terms without exposing
implementation details.

### 07 — Invitation

**Question answered:** What can I do next?

Keep “Contribution begins with attention,” but make the primary invitation an
experience rather than repository inspection. The natural order is:

1. begin with a case;
2. bring a subject to the Workspace;
3. inspect the reference implementation.

This preserves the existing actions while matching the needs of a first-time
visitor.

## Proposed text changes

The following copy is intentionally short. It is a proposed editorial
replacement, not an expansion of the page.

### Opening

**Headline**

> Information already exists. Orientation is what is missing.

**Paragraph**

> NEXAH helps people see how information relates, where its boundaries are,
> and which directions remain open. Sources stay visible. Judgment stays
> Human.

### What is NEXAH?

**Kicker**

> The ecosystem

**Headline**

> NEXAH is a place for orientation.

**Paragraph**

> It is not another AI, search engine, or knowledge base. It connects research,
> a deterministic Core, and a Human-facing Workspace so people can understand
> relationships, limits, and possible directions.

### What is ORION?

**Kicker**

> The deterministic Core

**Headline**

> ORION makes structure inspectable.

**Paragraph**

> ORION creates certified structural orientation from bounded material. Its
> results are deterministic, traceable, and open to review. It does not decide
> what the material means or what a Human should do.

### What is NEXAHEDRON?

**Kicker**

> The Human-facing Workspace

**Headline**

> One person. One question. One bounded session.

**Paragraph**

> NEXAHEDRON is the reference implementation of the NEXAH experience. A Human
> encounters material, notices relationships and boundaries, reflects, and
> may leave with a Human-owned Orientation Record.

### Why Orientation?

Keep the current headline and paragraph:

> Finding is not the same as understanding.

> Search can locate a source. A summary can compress it. Orientation helps show
> where a subject belongs, what supports it, where it stops, and which next
> steps are meaningful.

### Invitation

**Kicker**

> Begin where you are

**Headline**

> Contribution begins with attention.

**Paragraph**

> Bring a question. Review a case. Notice a missing boundary. Make a connection
> clearer. Teach with it, translate it, or help another person find their way.

The existing three destinations should remain, but their order should place
“Begin with a case” first, “Bring your own subject” second, and “Inspect the
reference implementation” third.

## Visual recommendations

No redesign is needed.

The existing section spacing, headline scale, line lengths, cream/navy/ochre
palette, and principle table should remain. The proposed identity sections can
reuse the current numbered `about-statement` composition. Each should have one
number, one kicker, one headline, and one paragraph. This repetition will give
the page the rhythm of an exhibition without adding interface complexity.

The only visual risk is monotony if all identity sections have identical
vertical weight. Keep their paragraphs short and use the existing borders as
transitions. ORION and NEXAHEDRON do not need cards, badges, diagrams, or
technical schemas.

Do not add a new diagram for Version 1. The canonical ecosystem map already
lives in the Visitor Guide. Repeating it here would turn the About page back
into documentation. A quiet text link to the Visitor Guide after the
NEXAHEDRON section is sufficient if an ecosystem overview is needed.

On narrow screens, preserve the current single-column reading order. Section
numbers should remain secondary to the headlines, and the three invitation
links should remain full-width, sequential choices.

## Final recommendation

Do not ship the current About page unchanged.

Recommend one final editorial revision before Version 1. The page is already
visually mature and constitutionally sound, but it does not yet perform its
primary public task: a new visitor cannot explain NEXAH, ORION, and NEXAHEDRON
after reading it.

The revision should add only the three short identity sections, preserve the
existing “Why Orientation?” and principles sections, and reorder the existing
invitation links. No new visual system, diagram, feature, or architectural
language is required.

After that revision, a visitor should be able to summarize the ecosystem in
one minute:

> NEXAH is the Orientation Ecosystem. ORION is its certified deterministic
> Core. NEXAHEDRON is the Human-facing Workspace where a person can undertake
> one bounded Orientation Session while keeping judgment and the final record
> Human-owned.
