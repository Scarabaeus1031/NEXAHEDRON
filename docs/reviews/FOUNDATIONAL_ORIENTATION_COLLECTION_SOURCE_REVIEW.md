## What is Evidence?

### NEXAH material carried forward

- ORIENTATION SCIENCE: `PART III — CURRENT EVIDENCE`, `13 What We Know`,
  `14 What We Don't Know Yet`, and `18 Benchmarks & Open Data`.
- THE CARTOGRAPHY LABORATORY: `06 Validation Laboratory` and its visible
  separation of hypothesis, testing, reproducibility, and evidence.
- THE ATLAS OF ATLASES: repeated `Evidence Zone` organization and separation of
  observed, compared, and open material.
- THE VISITOR’S GUIDE: the instruction that a Library is a map rather than a
  checklist, preserving Human inspection and choice.

### Editorial choice

The strongest recurring contribution was not a new definition of evidence. It
was a visual discipline: what is known, how it is supported, and what remains
open occupy different places. The case retains this separation.

It does not retain broad scientific-validity claims made by exploratory
plates. General claims are instead bounded by public National Academies and
metrology sources. The case defines evidence relationally—source, method,
claim, and boundary—because a source alone cannot establish what it bears on.

### Visual reuse

The semantic source → method → relationship → claim → boundary sequence is a
new accessible composition distilled from the existing Evidence Zone and
Validation Laboratory structures. A later facsimile treatment could place the
ORIENTATION SCIENCE `Current Evidence / Open Research Questions` spread beside
it, provided the source edition and exploratory status remain visible.

## What is a Map?

### NEXAH material carried forward

- MAP ATLAS: `11 — The Map of Maps`, especially map as model, layer, narrative,
  interface, selection, and view rather than world.
- NEXAH ATLAS — A Cartography of Perspectives: `Foreword — A Lens, Not a Final
  Theory`, `The Map Is Not the Territory`, and the closing unfinished-atlas
  plate.
- THE LANGUAGE ATLAS: guided non-linear organization of fields, perspectives,
  Operators, and paths.
- THE CARTOGRAPHY LABORATORY: the distinction among model, map, orientation,
  and prediction.
- `ATLAS_REPRESENTATIONS.md`: territory is a useful representation in some
  reconstructions but not a universal form.

### Editorial choice

The case begins with the International Cartographic Association's geographic
definition. This keeps map, symbol, selection, scale, purpose, and spatial
relationship concrete before extending map language to concepts or systems.

The phrase “every map is a choice” is preserved as an editorial relationship,
not expanded into the stronger claim that choices create reality. Map and
territory remain distinct; usefulness does not require completeness or
neutrality.

### Visual reuse

The territory → purpose → selection → symbol and scale → map diagram directly
distills `The Map of Maps`. The existing plate is the strongest candidate for
future side-by-side archival presentation because it already makes layers,
omission, and perspective visible without requiring a long caption.

## What is Scale?

### NEXAH material carried forward

- THE ATLAS OF ATLASES: `Transition I — Human to Microscopic`, the Cosmic
  Orientation Field, and the explicit movement between worlds.
- NEXAH — Maps of Human Reality: `The Fractal Map of Reality` and `Moment in a
  Continuous Field`, which stage several time and spatial scales together.
- NEXAH ATLAS — A Cartography of Perspectives: `The NEXAH Scale Map` and
  `The Universal Breather` as visual examples of cross-scale comparison.
- FIELD ATLAS I — WATER: `Synthesis — The 4 + 1 View`, demonstrating that
  complementary windows can reveal different aspects of one dynamic subject.

### Editorial choice

The visual corpus often proposes that one topology or pattern repeats across
all scales. The local review corpus does not establish that universal claim.
The case therefore preserves the movement among scales but rejects resemblance
as proof of shared cause or mechanism.

Cartographic scale provides the first exact relationship: representation to
ground. Spatial extent, temporal span, resolution, and organizational level
are then introduced as distinct extensions rather than collapsed into one
universal geometry.

### Visual reuse

The five-level detail → local pattern → system → region → planet sequence uses
the existing multi-world atlas rhythm. `Transition I — Human to Microscopic`
is the best future facsimile candidate. The speculative universal-topology
plates should remain research context, not introductory evidence.

## What is a Scientific Model?

### NEXAH material carried forward

- ORIENTATION SCIENCE: `03 From Models to Maps`, `11 Atlas Reconstruction`,
  `12 Navigation in Complex Systems`, and the separate `What We Know / What We
  Don't Know Yet` plates.
- THE CARTOGRAPHY LABORATORY: `Why Models Are Not Enough`, orientation versus
  prediction, and the Validation Laboratory.
- NEXAH ATLAS — A Cartography of Perspectives: `A Lens, Not a Final Theory`.
- THE LANGUAGE BOOK: the sentence as a small complete model of change and the
  distinction between state and transformation.
- `MODEL_RELATIONSHIP_MAP.md`: overlap, tension, compression, and
  incompatibility without forcing models into one derivation tree.

### Editorial choice

The case does not repeat “models are not enough” as a rejection of models.
Models are indispensable representations with explicit purposes and domains
of validity. Orientation begins by seeing their assumptions, comparison with
observation, and limits.

The scientific modeling loop is grounded with National Academies guidance and
a concrete NOAA weather-model example. NEXAH's map language contributes the
boundary: a useful representation remains different from the represented
phenomenon.

### Visual reuse

The phenomenon → question → representation → output → comparison sequence
combines the existing `From Models to Maps` composition with the Validation
Laboratory loop. A future archival spread could show those two plates together
to make the distinction between representation and validation visible.

## Shared editorial decisions

### Preserved

- the visual movement from isolated information to explicit relationship;
- maps as bounded perspectives rather than destinations;
- scale as a change in what becomes visible;
- evidence and unknowns occupying equally deliberate positions;
- continuation as a next path rather than a conclusion;
- the recurring language of field, map, boundary, and orientation where it
  clarifies rather than mystifies.

### Not carried into canonical cases

- universal physical or metaphysical claims unsupported by independent
  evidence;
- visual resemblance treated as proof of common mechanism;
- exploratory maturity presented as scientific consensus;
- “models versus maps” as a false choice;
- authority, confidence, evidence, and truth collapsed into one status;
- language that assigns meaning or decision to the system rather than the
  Human.

### Why the existing bitmap plates are not embedded

The selected plates combine strong visual thinking with dense OCR text and, in
several cases, exploratory claims outside the scope of the foundational case.
Embedding a whole plate would make those claims appear canonical and would be
difficult to read accessibly at mobile size.

The implementation therefore reuses the relationship grammar as semantic HTML
and CSS. Every diagram has a linear reading order and complete textual
equivalent. The original Works remain linked and versioned in the Library;
future facsimile use can be reviewed plate by plate rather than silently
cropped from its editorial context.
