# The NEXAHEDRON Website Experience

- Current status: Historical / partially superseded experience blueprint
- Previous role: broad public-experience proposal
- Current authority: none beyond adopted NEXAHEDRON presentation boundaries;
  D1–D6 govern current public alignment

The public website is a place for finding one's bearings.

It should feel like entering a museum, a library and an observatory at the same
time:

- a museum, because every object has been placed with care;
- a library, because many paths into knowledge remain open;
- an observatory, because visitors are invited to look closely and notice
  relationships they could not see before.

The experience is not organized around explaining the system. It is organized
around a Human arriving with curiosity.

## The person who arrives

Someone may arrive with a clear question.

Someone else may have a document, an idea, a half-remembered concept or a
subject they have always wanted to understand.

They may be a researcher, student, teacher, builder, artist, curator or simply
curious. They may know a great deal about one field and very little about
another. They may not yet know the right words for what they are looking for.

The website should not ask them to identify a role before they can begin.

It should meet them with one quiet recognition:

> You do not need a perfect question to begin finding your bearings.

## The emotional journey

The website should create a gradual movement.

### Wonder

The visitor first senses that there is more to see than a list of answers.

Wonder comes from spaciousness, a carefully chosen question, an unexpected
connection or a map that suggests unexplored paths. It should never come from
spectacle.

### Curiosity

The visitor sees a subject they recognize or a relationship they want to
follow. The site offers several clear beginnings without demanding an immediate
commitment.

Curiosity needs room. It should not be interrupted by urgency, pop-ups or a
request to create an account.

### Clarity

The visitor understands what the place is for, what they are looking at and
what they can do next.

Labels are plain. Evidence is distinguishable from explanation. Unknowns and
boundaries are named. Navigation does not require knowledge of the project.

### Confidence

Confidence grows because the experience is inspectable.

The visitor can see where a claim came from, what remains uncertain and why a
continuation is being offered. The site does not ask for trust through tone or
polish alone.

Confidence should remain proportionate. The aim is not to make every visitor
feel certain. It is to help them understand what their confidence can rest on.

### Invitation

The visitor feels able to continue: open another case, inspect evidence, follow
a connection, bring their own subject or help improve the Library.

The invitation is always optional. Stopping after one useful orientation is a
complete experience.

## The journey in moments

The website should be understood through moments rather than a sequence of
pages.

### Arrival

> I understand what kind of place this is.

### Recognition

> I can begin with something I care about.

### Orientation

> I can see the subject, its relationships, evidence and limits.

### Exploration

> I can choose a meaningful path without losing where I came from.

### Continuation

> I know what could come next, and the choice remains mine.

### Contribution

> I can help make a future orientation clearer for someone else.

No moment should require the visitor to understand internal architecture.

## The homepage

The homepage answers only three questions.

### What is this?

NEXAHEDRON is the Orientation Laboratory: a place to understand subjects through
context, relationships, evidence, uncertainty and possible next steps.

The answer should be short enough to grasp without scrolling through a theory
of the project.

### Why does it exist?

Information is abundant. Orientation is often missing.

The homepage should express this in a few grounded sentences. It does not need
to compare itself with every other tool or explain every future possibility.

### Where can I begin?

Offer immediate, concrete paths:

- explore a featured Orientation Case;
- begin with a question;
- enter a foundational collection;
- discover an unexpected connection.

The homepage is complete when the visitor can answer the three questions and
choose a beginning. Everything else belongs deeper in the site.

It should not contain a compressed version of every page, a long feature list
or an architectural diagram.

## Primary navigation

Navigation should be organized around Orientation:

- **Home** — return to the place of arrival;
- **Explore** — encounter cases, questions and paths;
- **Cases** — browse complete Orientation Cases;
- **Library** — enter the connected landscape;
- **About** — understand the purpose, principles and people behind the work;
- **GitHub** — inspect and contribute to the public project.

These names should remain stable and unsurprising.

Internal architecture, contract names, development phases and governance
language do not belong in primary navigation. They can be discovered from About
or GitHub by visitors who want to understand how the work is built and
protected.

At every depth, a visitor should be able to see:

- where they are;
- what they are exploring;
- how to return;
- why the current connection exists;
- which paths are available next.

## Explore

Explore is the heart of the public website.

It should open with Orientation Cases, not a description of the Library. A
visitor should encounter real subjects immediately.

### Featured Cases

Featured cases are carefully chosen entrances.

They should represent different domains, kinds of evidence and ways of
thinking. A case is featured because it creates a strong first orientation—not
because it is popular, new or promotional.

Every featured case should show its Human intention and one reason it may be a
useful place to begin.

### Newest

Newest cases show that the Library is living.

Recency is descriptive, not a signal of authority. New cases should remain
clearly marked by their editorial maturity. The newest item should never
automatically dominate the experience.

### Foundations

Foundations offer durable entry points: evidence, maps, energy, proof, systems,
time, language, life, Earth and other ideas that support many later
orientations.

They should feel like open rooms, not a curriculum everyone must finish in
order.

### Bridges

Bridges reveal meaningful connections across fields.

A visitor may follow measurement from Science into Mathematics, networks from
Computing into Society or rhythm from Music into Time. Every bridge should say
what is shared and where the connection stops.

### Collections

Collections gather cases around a sustained editorial intention.

They should offer a few strong entrances and visible routes, not long shelves
whose size suggests importance.

### Random Discovery

Random discovery creates room for surprise.

It should present one complete, reviewed possibility with enough context to
choose it. It is not an infinite feed, and it should not learn to maximize
clicks. The visitor can ask for another possibility or return to their previous
position.

### Search by Question

Let visitors begin in their own words.

The result should lead to cases that address the question, establish useful
context or make an unresolved boundary visible. It should not manufacture a
direct answer in place of a case.

### Search by Relationship

Let visitors look for causes, contrasts, dependencies, transformations,
patterns, systems and other forms of connection.

Results should explain why each relationship is present.

### Search by Evidence

Let visitors follow measurement, documents, maps, datasets, experiments,
artifacts, testimony or derivations.

Evidence search should keep source identity, authority and limitations visible.
Frequency of use is not a measure of truth.

## Orientation Cases

Every case should feel calm enough to think inside.

The page begins with the title and Human intention. It makes the starting
orientation visible before moving into subject and context. Relationships
should be easy to perceive without turning the case into a diagram of
everything.

Reading should feel deliberate:

- lines are comfortable;
- headings establish position;
- space separates different kinds of thought;
- tables and diagrams appear only when they clarify a relationship;
- source markers remain visible without interrupting every sentence;
- the reader can move between explanation and evidence without losing place.

### Evidence

Evidence should never feel hidden behind a disclosure, tooltip or technical
view.

A reader should be able to see:

- what source is being used;
- what it bears on;
- whether it supports, counters, contextualizes or limits;
- who owns it;
- which version is present;
- what access or uncertainty remains.

The page may keep the reading flow simple while ensuring that inspection is
always one clear action away.

### Assumptions and boundaries

Assumptions and boundaries are not footnotes added after the conclusion. They
belong to the visible orientation.

The reader should not need specialist attention to discover that a case cannot
support the decision they hoped to make.

### Unknowns

Unknowns should feel like honest frontiers.

Their visual treatment may be open and spacious, but never vague. Each unknown
should state what remains open and why it matters. An unknown is not a broken
page or a promise that an answer will arrive soon.

### Continuations

Continuation paths should be few, meaningful and explained.

Each path should say:

- what it opens;
- why it follows;
- what remains from the present orientation;
- what new material or perspective it introduces.

Continuation should feel like walking through a doorway, not clicking a
recommendation carousel.

At the end of the case, the reader should also be free to stop.

## The Library

The Library should feel like an atlas.

Collections become rooms.

Cases become maps.

Connections become paths.

Unknowns become frontiers.

Evidence becomes visible terrain.

The widest view offers a quiet sense of the landscape. It should not attempt to
show every node at once. A visitor can move closer to a collection, case,
relationship or source. At every scale, the current focus remains legible.

The Library should support several forms of movement:

- enter through a subject;
- follow a question;
- trace shared evidence;
- compare perspectives;
- move across a bridge;
- travel through time;
- explore a map;
- approach an unknown frontier;
- return to the path already taken.

A living map is not valuable because it is animated. It is valuable because it
makes relationships and choices visible.

Visual exploration must never be the only route. The same landscape should be
available through readable lists, clear headings, relationship descriptions and
complete keyboard navigation.

## Visual language

The visual atmosphere is quiet, precise and generous.

### White space

Space is part of the orientation. It separates subjects, evidence, uncertainty
and action. It gives the reader time to recognize a change in meaning.

White space should not become emptiness without purpose. Every interval helps
establish hierarchy or pause.

### Calm typography

Typography should feel editorial rather than promotional.

Large type establishes important moments. Comfortable reading type supports
long attention. Labels remain restrained. Weight, size and placement carry
hierarchy before color does.

### Soft transitions

Movement should preserve spatial understanding.

A case opening, a map drawing closer or a connection becoming visible may use
subtle transitions to show continuity. Motion never performs excitement. It
respects people who prefer or require reduced movement.

### Maps and constellations

Maps express position. Constellations express related points without pretending
the paths are physical. Both should be used only when their relationships are
named and accessible.

### Field lines

Field lines may suggest influence, direction or continuity. They should remain
subtle and never imply a measured force when the connection is conceptual.

### Subtle motion

Motion may reveal a path, a change of scale or a continuation. It should never
become ambient noise, reward or urgency.

### Restraint

The website should have:

- no dashboard feeling;
- no startup feeling;
- no social media feeling;
- no information overload;
- no endless feed;
- no notification pressure;
- no popularity counters;
- no decorative claim of intelligence;
- no interface that resembles chat by default.

The visual language should make careful attention feel natural.

## Editorial feeling

Visitors should feel that every page was placed deliberately.

Nothing exists merely to increase page count.

Every case has an intention.

Every connection has a reason.

Every source has a role.

Every unknown has a boundary.

Every continuation has a direction.

Collections should be small enough to understand. Featured material should be
selected by editorial purpose. Gaps should remain visible rather than filled
with weak pages.

The site should be comfortable saying:

- this case is still being reviewed;
- this evidence is unavailable;
- these perspectives disagree;
- this subject has no case yet;
- this orientation stops here.

Care is visible not through perfection, but through honest placement.

## About

About should offer depth gradually.

The first layer explains why Orientation matters and what NEXAH is in ordinary
language. A visitor who wants to go deeper can find the Manifesto, public story,
evaluation approach, contribution guide and technical source.

The site should not require those documents before a person can experience a
case.

Architecture is discoverable for people who want to inspect it. It is not a
threshold at the entrance.

## Community

Contribution should be discovered through use.

A reader notices a missing source. A teacher sees a clearer way to introduce a
case. A researcher identifies an uncertainty. A designer imagines a better map.
Only then does the invitation to help become meaningful.

People can help Orientation grow through:

- review;
- evidence inspection;
- new Orientation Cases;
- translations;
- visualizations;
- case connections;
- teaching material;
- accessibility;
- reproducible critique.

The website should not suggest that everyone can edit everything immediately.
It should explain that public cases are carefully reviewed and that different
contributions carry different responsibilities.

Contribution invitations belong near the context they concern:

- report an evidence issue beside the evidence;
- propose a connection beside the case paths;
- help translate a reviewed case from the case itself;
- find editorial frontiers in the Library;
- inspect the full project through GitHub.

Community should feel like stewardship, not content production.

## Never overwhelm

At every moment, ask:

- What is the visitor trying to understand?
- What is the one most useful thing to see now?
- Which context must remain visible?
- Which actions can wait?
- Which complexity carries meaning and must not be hidden?

Progressive depth is not concealment. The essential orientation remains visible;
detail becomes available when the visitor chooses it.

Avoid presenting:

- every collection on the homepage;
- every connection on one map;
- every evidence field at the same visual weight;
- every continuation as equally important;
- every contributor path before the visitor has experienced a case.

Calm comes from clear choice, not from removing meaningful information.

## Long-term vision

The website should slowly become a place people return to when they want to
understand something.

Not because it contains all knowledge.

Not because it always provides an answer.

Because it helps them pause before going deeper elsewhere.

They may arrive with a paper and leave ready to inspect its evidence. They may
arrive with a historical question and leave able to compare perspectives. They
may arrive with curiosity and leave with a more precise unknown.

The website succeeds when the visitor can say:

> I know where I am.

> I can see what supports this view.

> I understand where it stops.

> I know where I might continue.

That is the spirit of nexahedron.com.
