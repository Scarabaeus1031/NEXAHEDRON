# NEXAH Version 1.0 Public Release Claim

- Status: Canonical
- Version: 1.0
- Purpose: Public release claim
- Publication condition: Applies to the coordinated NEXAH Version 1.0 release

## 1. What is NEXAH Version 1.0?

NEXAH Version 1.0 is the first coordinated public release of an orientation
system designed to help people work with questions, sources, relationships,
boundaries, uncertainty, and provenance without transferring Human authority
to software. It brings together the NEXAH framework, the Orientation Language,
the Open Orientation Protocol, the certified ORION core, the NEXAH Library,
and NEXAHEDRON as a Human-facing reference implementation. These parts retain
separate responsibilities and versions; their inclusion in one release does
not merge their authority.

## 2. What problem does it solve?

People often investigate one question across documents, books, datasets, maps,
repositories, conversations, and software systems. Access to those materials
does not by itself preserve which source or version was considered, which
fragments mattered, why two things were related, what remained uncertain, or
who accepted a proposal. NEXAH provides a shared orientation structure for
keeping those distinctions visible while the Human remains responsible for
meaning, judgment, continuation, Rest, and action.

## 3. What can a visitor experience?

A visitor can use `nexah.de` to understand the project, inspect its protocol
and governance, and enter its Library and publications. In NEXAHEDRON, the
visitor can begin with one genuine question, encounter one real source, keep
exact fragments with their locations and provenance, create explicit
relationships, write a Reflection, and decide when to Rest. The completed or
deliberately rested session produces an inspectable Orientation Record that
distinguishes Human-authored material, encountered source material,
relationships, uncertainty, provenance, and the final Rest boundary.

## 4. What does Version 1.0 intentionally not do?

Version 1.0 does not present NEXAH as a source of truth, search engine,
assistant, recommendation authority, or decision-making system. It does not
claim that an Orientation Record is complete or correct, that every domain or
source format is supported, or that software can determine what an orientation
means to a person. It does not require automated reasoning, generated
language, persistent shared Memory, automatic learning, or automatic evolution
of the Orientation Language. Unknown, unavailable, unsupported, and uncertain
states remain visible rather than being replaced with inferred answers.

## 5. Why is NEXAHEDRON only a reference implementation?

NEXAHEDRON demonstrates one way to implement the Open Orientation Protocol
through a Human-facing Workspace, Encounter, Reflection, Rest, and an optional
boundary to a deterministic orientation kernel. It owns its interaction,
presentation, and local session flow, but it does not own the protocol,
Orientation Language, Library authority, ORION authority, source identity, or
Human meaning. Its routes, components, visual language, and technology choices
are not requirements for other implementations.

## 6. What should a visitor expect?

After reading the Position Paper and completing one Orientation Session, a
visitor should understand what NEXAH is responsible for, what each
participating system is allowed to contribute, and where Human authority
remains. The visitor should be able to inspect the question, source, selected
fragments, relationships, boundaries, uncertainty, provenance, and Rest state
without treating the result as a final answer. Version 1.0 proves one bounded,
reproducible orientation process whose claims and limits can be examined.
